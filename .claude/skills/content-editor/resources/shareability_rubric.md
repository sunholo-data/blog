# Shareability Rubric

Use this to evaluate whether a post is worth sharing. For each criterion, quote a specific line from the draft as evidence — never give abstract feedback.

Score each: ✅ strong / ⚠️ weak / ❌ missing.

## 1. Hook (first 2 sentences)

Do the opening sentences earn the next paragraph? A reader should feel compelled, intrigued, or destabilised — not introduced to a topic.

**Strong:** begins with a concrete image, a contrarian claim, a specific moment, or a question that reframes the reader's assumption.

**Weak signals:**
- Opens with "In today's fast-paced world..." or any abstract scene-setting
- States the topic instead of the stake ("This post is about X")
- Buries the interesting claim in paragraph 3

**Test:** cover everything except the first two sentences. Would a stranger keep reading?

## 2. Stakes

Why should the reader care *now*? What changes if they understand this? What breaks if they don't?

**Weak signals:** the post could have been written any time in the last 5 years. No sense of urgency, trend, or specific trigger.

## 3. Specificity

One real anecdote beats three generalisations. Names, numbers, timestamps, actual error messages, actual code, actual conversations.

**Strong:** "I ran `ailang parse foo.ml` at 2am on a Tuesday and it crashed with..."
**Weak:** "Sometimes parsers have issues with complex inputs."

**Test:** count the concrete nouns vs abstract nouns in each paragraph. If abstract > concrete, tighten.

## 4. Counterintuitive angle

What does this post say that the reader wouldn't already guess from the title? The surprising, contrarian, or unexpected take is what gets shared.

**Weak signals:** the post confirms the reader's priors. Reads like consensus. Could be an AI summary of existing discourse.

**Test:** what's the ONE sentence in this post that would make someone argue with you at a dinner party?

## 5. One-sentence takeaway

Can a reader summarise the post in one sentence to a friend? If not, the argument is diffuse.

Write it out: "This post argues that ___."

## 6. Tweetable line

Is there a single line that's quotable on its own? Not a slogan — a sentence that earns a pull quote because it compresses the insight.

**Test:** scan for sentences under 15 words that could stand alone. If none, the prose is probably too hedged or too wordy.

## 7. Ending

Does the last paragraph land, or trail off with a weak "in conclusion" or a "what do you think?" that wasn't earned?

**Strong endings:**
- Return to the opening image with new meaning
- A prediction the reader can test
- A single-sentence reframe
- A concrete next action

**Weak endings:**
- Summary of what was just said
- Vague call to action
- "Only time will tell"

## Overall: the "share" test

Imagine the reader finishing and deciding whether to share. They will share if:
- It made them feel smarter (gave language to something they felt)
- It made them feel seen (named a frustration or observation accurately)
- It gave them a weapon (a sharp framing for an argument they're having)
- It introduced them to something genuinely new

They will NOT share a post that:
- Confirms the obvious
- Reads like corporate comms or AI slop
- Hedges every claim
- Has no single takeaway they can name
